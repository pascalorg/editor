- [x] 2.8 Phase 10: strength/maturity striking criterion beside the elapsed-time one, reporting which governs and the accumulated maturity; a criterion lacking its inputs falls back to time and says so — **done**, the strength criterion is a `curing` project decision (`maturityTargetDegreeHours`, the Nurse–Saul target calibrated from job-cured specimens, plus the datum it is measured against and the required strength it corresponds to as a fraction of the design strength, which names the criterion in a contract's words without deciding it) — never defaulted, because a guessed target is a check that cannot fail. `strikingTime` judges it against the elapsed-table answer and the later of the two governs, with `StrikingTime.criterion` (`'elapsed-time' | 'strength'`) and `StrikingMaturity` (target, datum, accumulated degree-hours at the strike date) carrying the verdict; at a constant recorded cure temperature the accumulation is `M_target / (t − T0)`. The three spec scenarios hold: strength governs when its target falls later than the table; time governs when it is the later of the two; and a stated criterion missing the temperature history (or a temperature at or below the datum, or a strength named with no maturity target behind it) falls back to the elapsed table and names the missing input as a `strength-criterion-not-evaluated` warning. It threads the same five surfaces as the rest of the striking work: the takeoff panel's Held-for section reads the governing criterion and accumulated maturity beside each period, the CSV's period rows carry the sentence in `governingRule`, both AI reads carry it the same way, and the fallback reaches every surface through `projectFormworkCaveats`. Shipped with the unchanged-figure discipline — a project stating none of the new inputs keeps the elapsed-only answer

- [x] 7.2 Charge an asset with a life per use, reporting the price, life, residual value and per-use figure; charge one without a life at today's internal recharge and name the basis
- [x] 7.3 Test that no owned item appears in any total at zero under either basis
- [x] 7.4 Report amortised and recharge lines as distinguishable, with the total stating it combines both bases
- [x] 7.5 Report a use count exceeding the stated life as an overrun, naming the replacement it implies — **shipped**, in `cost.ts`'s amortised branch: where a line's owned quantity (total fittings = total uses) exceeds the stated `expectedUses`, the charge stops being per use and becomes the replacement the overrun implies — `perUse × life + purchasePerUnit × ceil((uses − life) / life)` — so uses past the life are whole new assets at list rather than further uses of an asset that is spent. `CostLine.overrun` carries uses/life/replacements/replacementCost, `BomCost.overruns` collects them, and the caveat names the line, both counts and the money, ending with the purchase the overrun implies. Still owned-stock money outside `totalCost`, for the amortisation block's own reason
- [x] 7.6 Finance cost from a stated rate over the spend-to-recovery period, reported beside and outside the cash total; test the cash total is unchanged by its presence; no rate means no figure; the figure states its rate, period and any pours excluded for being undated — **shipped**, `financeRatePerAnnum` on the rate group (schema, settings resolution, patch write contract, settings panel) and `bomCost`'s optional schedule: the spend-to-recovery period is the programme's own span, first delivery to last release, because recovery is not modelled and a period the engine cannot read off stated dates would be a date it invented. `financeCost = totalCost × rate/100 × spanDays/365` lands on `BomCost` beside `totalCost`, never inside it — the caveat leads with the money and ends "outside the cash total", and the tests pin that the total is unchanged by its presence. No rate means no figure and no span means no figure; undated pours are excluded from the period and named ("1 of 2 pours have no date…")
